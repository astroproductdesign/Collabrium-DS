Hover peek card c-card-peek

A floating card that previews the row or list item under the pointer or keyboard focus, so a list view never makes you imagine the thing it lists. Point at a row and the card lands beside it. Move to the next row and it slides along, its content cross-fading. Move away and it goes after a short grace. One shell with two body fillings: a format sample (300px) and an audience segment (272px).

New component or variant?

Recommendation: a new Card variant, c-card-peek, with two body fillings

It is a Card by surface and a floating layer by behaviour, which is exactly how Ad Placement Card is built. It goes next to Ad Placement Card in the Card section, not under Tooltip and not inside Notes. The two fillings are modifiers of one class, not two components. This follows the scan's call: "same shell, same shadow, same motion; they differ in width and in what fills the body."

It is not a fourth Ad Placement Card filling. The shell is shared, but the placement model is different. Ad Placement Card follows the cursor across a map, takes no pointer input, and every trigger has its own static card. The peek anchors to a row and slides between rows. It is hoverable, and it is one instance whose content gets swapped. Merging them would put two placement and motion models behind one class name.

Later: if a third floating card turns up, pull the shared part (fixed at body level, z-index 500, opacity + visibility hide, spacing-4 rise, viewport cap, reduced-motion and forced-colours blocks) into one floating-card shell. Ad Placement and Peek would then be fillings of it. Not proposed now, because two instances are not yet a pattern.

Live demo

Point at a row, or Tab to a name. The card lands on first show, slides between rows with a cross-fade, and goes after a short grace. You can move the pointer onto the card and it stays. Event log

    Last click

    Nothing clicked yet.

    A click hides the peek right away, as in the source, and would open the item's detail view. Nothing navigates in this showcase.

    Phone view

    Rendered in Chrome at 390px on a touch device. The live peek is off on phones and touch screens: there is no hover, and a tap on a row opens its details. The rows already show everything the peek would, so nothing is lost. The cards themselves still render correctly at this width, for any host that shows them statically.

    Progress Bar fix: high-contrast mode

    The peek's channel bars use the shipped Progress Bar. In Windows high-contrast mode (forced colours) the browser removes background colours, so every Progress Bar in the system disappears, leaving only its labels. These are Chrome renders of the gallery's Progress Bar block in that mode. The fix, proposed for Progress Bar itself (not just inside the peek), keeps the track as an outlined box and keeps the fill:

    @media (forced-colors:active){ .c-progress-track{forced-color-adjust:none; background:Canvas; outline:1px solid CanvasText;} .c-progress-fill, .c-progress-stack-segment{forced-color-adjust:none; background:CanvasText;} }

    Plain fills take the system text colour. Toned fills (Complete, Error, the stacked budget segments) keep their tone colours, so the segments can still be told apart.

    Deliberate departure from Tooltip: Tooltip hides instantly. The peek waits duration-fast before hiding, because without the grace it can't be hoverable (1.4.13) or slide between rows. Script reads every duration from the tokens, so nothing is duplicated in JS.

    Positioning

    1. Beside the list, right: the bounds' right edge + spacing-12. The bounds are the table wrapper or list container. Vertically centred on the row, but never raised above the list's own top edge unless the viewport forces it. That way it can't climb over the section above when the row is near the bottom of the screen.
    2. Beside the list, left: left edge − spacing-12 − width, with the same vertical rule.
    3. Above the row: bottom edge spacing-8 above the trigger row. Right-aligned spacing-24 in from the row's right end.
    4. Below the row: top edge spacing-8 below the trigger row, same horizontal rule.
    5. Last resort, clamp: on whichever side of the row has more room, clamped to the spacing-12 viewport gutter. Only reached when the card is taller than the space both above and below the row.

    Every placement is finally clamped to the spacing-12 gutter, and max-height keeps the card inside it. Height is measured, not hard-coded (the source used a fixed 232px). No caret: the side and the row highlight already say which row the card belongs to.

    Why not the source's inset (over the row's right end): it covers the right-hand columns of the row being read, and the pointer's path to the card crosses other rows, which fights hoverability. Above and below sit relative to the trigger, so the card covers neighbouring rows of the same list, never unrelated content.

    Trigger and states

    Peek hiddenopacity 0, visibility:hidden, pointer-events:none, at the hidden pose.
    .is-visibleShown and hoverable (pointer-events:auto).
    .is-jumpingSet on first show only: land, don't slide.
    .is-switchingContent faded out while the next item mounts.
    tr.is-peekingTrigger row keeps Table's hover fill while its peek shows, including while the pointer sits on the card.
    .is-staticDocs, print and tests only. Lays the card out in normal flow.

    The peek has no hover, focus or disabled states of its own because it is never a control. The trigger keeps its host's full state set (Table row hover, the control's focus ring).

    Accessibility

    • Opens on hover and keyboard focus. Focus means :focus-visible on the trigger's own control (a real <button> in the row). A mouse click that moves focus doesn't open it a second time.
    • Dismissible: Escape hides it while it shows. The listener runs in the capture phase and stops propagation only then, so it doesn't also close a modal underneath. Focus stays on the trigger. The dismissal sticks until the pointer leaves that row or focus moves to another trigger.
    • Hoverable: while visible it takes the pointer. Moving onto the card cancels the hide grace.
    • Persistent: it stays while the trigger is hovered or focused, or the card itself is hovered. No timeout. The pointer never hides a keyboard-held card. While its trigger still has focus, a pointer leaving a row or the card doesn't close it. Pointer hides only act on the row that opened the card.
    • Supplementary only. aria-hidden="true", never focusable, no live region, focus never moves into it, no links or buttons inside. The trigger must already carry everything the peek shows (name, family, figures, channel values), as the demo rows do.
    • Why not role="tooltip" + aria-describedby (Ad Placement Card's route): one shared instance whose content swaps would make the description change under the reader. And the sample itself is visual.
    • Forced colours: edge, body rule and bars switch to CanvasText. The peeking row's cells (tr.is-peeking > td) get a Highlight outline, because the Neutral-2 fill is stripped. The outline is on the cells, not the tr, because row outlines are unreliable with border-collapse.

    Phone width and touch

    Decision: never shown on (hover:none) or at max-width:767px. CSS hides it and the script won't open it.

    • 767px is Table's own mobile breakpoint. The source's 760px is not used, so no new breakpoint is added.
    • Below that, a 300px card plus gutters covers most of the list. There is no "beside", and the inset fallback would hide the row being read.
    • Touch has no hover, and a tap opens the item's detail view, which already shows the full sample. A long-press peek would compete with scrolling.
    • Nothing is lost, because the trigger always carries the information (host contract). On phones the host may also show a small inline thumbnail in the row.

    Host contract

    • One peek instance per list, appended to <body>. An overflow scroller (the table's) would otherwise clip it.
    • Every trigger has a stable data-peek-id and contains a real control (a button that opens details).
    • The host fills the content. Format: slot child, name, family Tag. Audience: slot child, name, figure, three bar values. A live sample may start on show and stop on hide (the source plays it).
    • Scroll: pointer mode hides, and hover re-arms only after a real pointer move. The page sliding under a still pointer doesn't count, so the card can't flicker back on whatever row scrolls under the cursor. Keyboard mode re-places the card without sliding, at most once per animation frame, while the focused trigger is still on screen. That's needed because the browser's own scroll-into-view on Tab would otherwise close it straight away. This refines Tooltip's "scroll hides" rule.
    • One peek at a time. Showing a peek hides any other peek on the page.
    • Bar values are percentages, 0–100. Set the fill width with CardPeek.barWidth(value), which clamps to 0–100%. Print the value as given, or "—" with .is-empty when missing.

    Do / Don't

    Do
    • Keep one instance per list and swap its content. That's what makes the slide and cross-fade possible.
    • Put everything the peek shows in the trigger too.
    • Keep all three channel rows, in order, even when a value is missing.
    • Let the consumer own the slot content. The component's job ends at ratio and clip.
    Don't
    • Put links, buttons or anything focusable in the peek. Use a Popover or the detail view.
    • Use it as the only place information lives, or on touch.
    • Add a show delay. The card arrives as soon as the pointer or focus does.
    • Colour channel bars by element. Channels aren't departments, so the fill stays Obsidian.

    Open questions

    Decided: shadow-3 stays. Audience width stays 272px. Channel figures are percentages, 0–100, with the fill clamped. Hidden on (hover:none) and at max-width:767px.
