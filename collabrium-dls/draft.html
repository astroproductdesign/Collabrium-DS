Draft — Tutorial Modal

Proposed as a variant of Modal / dialog: a numbered walkthrough that spotlights one part of the surface at a time and explains it in a short card, flying out of whatever it is describing. Adapted from the supplied .tw-card markup — every hardcoded value is now a tokens.css token, and the panel recipe, the close button, the footer buttons and the scrim are all existing components.css parts reused as they ship. Nothing has been committed to components.css, DESIGN-SYSTEM.md or preview.html.

Round 2. Both buttons now sit right, 8px apart. The title is capped at two lines and the body at four, and neither truncates — a tour card is the whole surface, so cutting the copy would lose it with nowhere to read the rest. The caps are authoring maxima; over-writing grows the card, which the author can see and fix. No cap on step count. Section 3 shows both at the limit, together, and past it. Modal's own “no multi-step flows” Don’t gains a carve-out named to this variant specifically, so nothing else can cite it as precedent (section 7). Persistence and cross-surface steps are parked as out of scope — neither changes the UI.

2 — The card, static

The three shapes a step takes. Nothing else: a step is an intro, a stop, or the sign-off.

3 — Edges

Both maxima — two lines of title, four of body — at the limit, together, and past it. Nothing truncates: a tour card is the whole surface, so there is no row to expand and no tooltip to fall back on, and cutting the copy would simply lose it. Over-writing grows the card instead, which is a signal the author can see and act on rather than information the reader never gets. Then a one-step tour and the docked form the card takes below 768px.

4 — Where the card goes

Chosen by fit, never by preference: the card takes the first side of the target with room for it, tested in this order, and falls back to centred if none has room. A 16px gutter is held against every viewport edge.

7 — The rule this variant sits against

Modal / dialog's own Don't: “Don't use a modal for anything that isn't a focused, single decision — long forms or multi-step flows need a full page or panel, not a modal.”

A numbered tutorial is a multi-step flow. Either the rule gets a sentence, or this variant is quietly breaking the parent it belongs to.

Settled — the carve-out is named to this variant, and to nothing else. Modal's Don’t gains a sentence naming Tutorial Modal by name, rather than a general licence for “flows that don’t collect input”: “The one exception is the Tutorial Modal variant, whose numbered steps are reads rather than decisions — it collects no input, commits nothing, and is abandonable at any point with nothing lost. No other multi-step flow inherits this.” That keeps the rule doing its job for every other case and stops the next multi-step thing citing this one as precedent.
